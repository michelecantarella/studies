How the density is set

For each card, the fair alternative if it were offered there: for those still playing (counting the winning cards they may have found by then), staying costs the main cards left + (chance of not finding all of them) × the alternative, owed at the end; the fair alternative is the one equal to that cost, with the future cards counted at their density. The density decays from the first card as d0 · (ρ + (1 − ρ) · (1 + t/τ)−α) (a Pareto curve towards a floor ρ·d0), with ρ, τ, α fitted on each deck so that the fair alternative stays as close as possible to the one at the offer on every card. The fit may lengthen the alternative by at most the factor set on the left (a lower density later makes staying dearer, at the offer too).
With 1 winning card the best decay is none. With 2–3 the fair alternative must drop a little on the very first cards (no one can finish there), and the decay can only win it back later; the denser the start, the smaller that drop. Drift in the table = how far, on average and at most, the fair alternative moves away from the one at the offer (in cards, from the densities).

Simulation

Press Run simulation.

Alternative: median [10%–90%] of the alternative offered. Accepted: share of rolls whose alternative is within the bounds (the others are redrawn when "redraw" is on). Gap at 10 / 20: for those still playing at card 10 / 20, cost of staying minus the alternative, in cards (0 = as fair as at the offer; negative = staying has become better). Drift (max): how far the fair alternative (from the densities) moves away from the one at the offer, survivor-weighted mean and the largest, in cards. Switch better: share of offers where switching is better at the offer. End: chance of reaching the last card without all the winning cards (then the alternative is owed). In window: chance that all the winning cards fall within the calibration window (target = p_inside). Density 20: density of card 20 relative to the initial one.
