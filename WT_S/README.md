## The task

- **Cards**: orange (can be the winning card) or white. One winning card per sequence, equally likely on any orange card; the last card is orange. Emoji: faces (cards and the alternative), objects (keys).
- **Every card is locked** and is opened with a **key**, in order, white ones too. Unlocking a card shows whether it is the winning card. A **key** is one card in the keys bar; keys are unlimited.
- **The screen**: three bars, one above the other — the cards, the keys, the alternative. Each shows its sequence as a row of tiles (the three rows line up), a banner (what it is doing, or what a click on it does: solid colour = a button), and its count on the right (Cards unlocked x/total, Keys available, Alternative cards). The bar being played opens to show its deck. Under the bars: the offer with its countdown, the Submitting countdown.
- **Before the offer**: the keys gifted (if any) are already in hand, shown as **Starting keys** next to the reward on the briefing screen; in full and mixed, then the keys still to make, one key card each, before the offer (x = 2, 4, 6, 8 or 10, equally likely, not told). Truncated and truncated + gift: the offer at the start.
- **The offer**: all three bars close; under them "Cards are available: unlock or switch" with a 10–30 s countdown; the two buttons pulse. Click the cards bar (unlock) or the alternative bar (switch). The countdown running out unlocks too.
- **After the offer** (2026-11-05): at the offer the participant clicks the cards bar (unlock), the keys bar (make keys) or the alternative (switch); the countdown running out makes keys. From then on the bar not being played is a button: a click changes the mode from the next card. Unlocking, the cards are unlocked in order, one key each; a card with no key left gets a key card first, automatically ("No keys left: making one"). Keys made by choice while a key is in hand are inefficient (logged: `keys_inefficient*`).
- **Switching**: a confirmation over the alternative's bar ("Switch to the alternative? This choice can't be undone."); the card being played keeps running underneath, and an ending it reaches is shown only on Keep going. After switching the other bars are greyed.
- **Training** (16 screens, in the order a sequence is played): a card in the keys deck, one key already in hand, and a practice round that earns a second one (1–2), the two ways to win: the main deck (the winning card among the orange cards, every card locked) (3) and the alternative deck (4), the offer (5), unlocking with the two keys: a white card, an orange card (6–7), no keys left: the game switches between the decks by itself (8), switching (9), the alternative (10), the panels: sequences, reward, penalties, giving up (11–14), ready (15), comprehension check (16). The instructions box sits above the board, sticky at the top of the window (always in view).

## Design

- **Quartets, final (2026-11-05)**: a truncated baseline and three treated versions, each design played once in each by four participants (`Seeds` sheet), one of each per participant plus one at random. **trunc**: no keys, one deck. **full** (A): x keys made; with chance 20% extra keys gifted too (x × Beta(0.5, 0.5), at least 1). **mixed** (B): keys gifted and made, always (as below). **trunc_gift** (C, the inverse of A): x keys gifted; with chance 20% extra keys to make too. The three treated versions show a choice of 2 or 3 decks in half the sequences. On 2,000 draws: full 6.0 made / 0.6 gifted, mixed 3.4 / 3.0, trunc_gift 0.7 / 6.1 (means). Simulated power with 2,000 participants (base 20%, offer only → every decision): keys in hand 83–98%, effort (made vs gifted) 52–81%, deck choice 79–100% (−3 to −5 pp). Every key still comes with its extra white card, so the decision at the offer is the same in all four.
- **Quartets** (earlier on 2026-11-05): each design is played in four versions by four different participants (`Seeds` sheet, one row per design, a status per version). **full**: x keys made (sunk effort, perceived discount). **mixed**: g keys gifted, m made, drawn independently (keys in hand and effort off the diagonal: the shape of both effects and their interaction; starting with keys does not tell that the offer comes at once). **trunc**: the truncated deck, no keys. **trunc_gift**: the full version with all its x keys gifted, the offer at the start (the same keys in hand, no effort). The keys, made or gifted, look like a discount but are not one: an extra white card in front of each covered pair. Contrasts: trunc_gift − trunc = keys in hand with no effort (opportunities to waste); full − trunc_gift = the same keys, made or gifted (sunk effort); full − trunc = as WT_Q. Each participant plays one of each version and one at random, in random order. **Deck choice**, a factor of its own, the same in every version: half the sequences show one deck, the rest 2 or 3 (equally likely), in random order, to choose from. The decks are designs from the pool open in that version (claimed together), completed with new designs when the pool has too few; the decks not chosen go back to the pool, a new one chosen is added to it. (In WT_L and WT_N, where only full versions had a choice, switching at the offer was 13–20 points lower with a choice: small samples, the same direction.)
- **Pairs** (as WT_Q): the **full** version makes x keys before the offer (the sunk effort). The keys look like a discount but are not one: they come with x/2 extra white cards to unlock. The **truncated** version plays a new deck without them (`truncDeck`): each pair covered by two keys (extra white card, then the card) becomes one card (a key, then the card), no keys, the offer right away. Every orange card costs the same to reach in both: the same effort expected at the offer and after each orange card found empty, the same best move (checked: identical on 2,000 designs).
- **The deck** (WT_Q's generator): drawn in slots of two cards of work from the offer on; the first x/2 slots are two cards (an extra white card, then the slot's card: both covered by the x keys), the later ones one card (a key and the card). The white card comes first, as in WT_Q, else an orange card first in its pair would cost 1 to reach in the full version and 2 in the truncated one. Flat effort expected: E ~ U(10, 17), within ±15%. 16–30 cards, at least 5 orange, no white run as long as the alternative.
- **The alternative**: set at the offer from three break-even values (staying, switching allowed later, against switching), by the power mean of the cost of staying, each with the switching policy optimal under its own criterion: geometric (lower bound), arithmetic (the fair value) and quadratic (upper bound). Half the designs draw it uniformly in the half of [geometric, fair] nearer the fair value (switching better), half in the half of [fair, quadratic] nearer it (switching worse), rounded strictly on its side: switching best at the offer in exactly 50%. On 2,000 designs: geometric 0.62 × fair, quadratic 1.51 × fair (medians); the alternative 0.89 × fair in the lower half, 1.15 × in the upper (5–95%: 0.79–1.30); 2 cards from the fair value (median, 90%: 3).
- **The fair value** (`altFixed(st, 1)`, on the deck drawn): with the orange cards o₁ … o_M left, C_j the work from now to reach o_j (the winning card equally likely on each), A\* = min_j [Σ_{l≤j} C_l + (M − j) C_j] / j: the least, over "check j more orange cards, then switch", of the work expected; j = M is never switching, whose value is the **effort expected** (always staying). So fair ≤ effort expected, equal when checking a few cards and then switching is never cheaper (at the offer: 0.84 × the effort expected, median; 10–90%: 0.71–0.98). Equivalently, by backward induction: V_{n+1} = A, V_i = min{A, c_i + (1 − h_i) V_{i+1}}, A\* the A at which staying (to the next orange card, then V) costs A. When the alternative is at or above the effort expected at every later point, switching is never the best move and staying with switching allowed costs the same as always staying (`quartet_lab.html`: red = blue).
- **The fair value moves along the deck** (by design, not by chance): the effort expected (always staying) is kept flat (±15%), but the value of checking a few more cards and then switching shrinks as the orange cards run out, so the fair value rises towards the effort expected: before the last orange card it is the work to reach it, 1.17 × the fair value at the offer (median; 10–90%: 0.91–1.50). The alternative is fixed, so it becomes relatively more convenient further on: in 77% of the designs where staying is best at the offer, switching becomes best at some later point. "Switching better / worse" (`alt_good`) holds at the offer only; later decisions are judged on the best move there (`switch_best` per card, `stay_cost` per card, `fair_at_exit`, `switch_gain_at_exit`). No orange-card distribution avoids it: on a deck the participant sees, the fair value stays the same after every orange card only if the next one comes exactly A/m cards on (m = the orange cards left), a fixed placement, which with cards in slots of 2 has an exact solution only for 3 orange cards (A = 12: 4, 6, 12 cards) or 4 (A = 24); WT_S has at least 5. `fair_lab.html` shows it on 2,000 decks.
- **The model** (`bestMove`): the cards covered by a key cost 1, the others 2 (a key, then the card). The game always follows this path (keys only when none is left); `keys_at_exit` = keys left unused.

## Simulated (2,000 sequences)

Full deck 16–30 cards (median 21), truncated 12–26 (median 18), alternative 6–20 (median 12); switching best at the offer in 50% (= the half with power < 1); x uniform over 2–10. Full vs truncated: the effort expected (staying, and with switching) after each orange card, the best move and the alternative's fixed point differ by 0.
